\section*{Target Venues}
Primary \href{https://www.sigsac.org/ccs.html}{ACM CCS}; also \href{https://www.usenix.org/conferences/byname/108}{USENIX Security}, \href{https://www.ieee-security.org/}{IEEE S\&P}, \href{https://www.ndss-symposium.org/}{NDSS}, and the journal \href{https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/tq}{IEEE TDSC}. Fit: a redesigned \emph{defensive} multi-agent architecture that tolerates compromised insiders and adaptive attackers matches the applied-security scope of the conferences and the dependability/fault-tolerance scope of TDSC.

\section*{Motivation}
Multi-agent defense pipelines now filter LLM outputs. AutoDefense (arXiv:2403.04783) chains an Input agent, an Intention Analyzer, an Original-Prompt Analyzer, and a Judge under a Coordinator (optional Llama-Guard fourth agent, then an Output agent), and reduces jailbreak attack success rate --- reported, e.g., from 55.7\% to 7.95\% with a LLaMA-2-13b three-agent configuration. But the \emph{defense is itself a multi-agent system} whose own security is unexamined: it assumes every internal defender is honest; its Coordinator is a single trust/aggregation point with no voting, quorum, or Byzantine tolerance; it feeds the \emph{untrusted} response straight to its judges (a second-order prompt-injection surface \emph{against the defenders}); it is tested only against static attacks; and it targets \emph{jailbreak} (harmful content), not prompt \emph{injection} (task-integrity violations that can look benign). A single hardened model (SecAlign, arXiv:2410.05451) has no colluding co-defender and no compromised coordinator --- those threats appear only when the defense is a team.

\section*{Research Gap}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AutoDefense (arXiv:2403.04783)} --- multi-agent response filter; assumes honest agents, a single Coordinator with no Byzantine tolerance, feeds untrusted content to judges, is tested only statically, and defends jailbreak, not injection/task-integrity.
  \item \textbf{Krum (Blanchard et al., NeurIPS 2017)} --- Byzantine-robust selection with tolerance condition $2f+2<n$, but for gradient aggregation under a \emph{trusted} master.
  \item \textbf{SecAlign (arXiv:2410.05451) and StruQ (arXiv:2402.06363)} --- injection-hardened \emph{single} models (preference optimization; structured-input/delimiter separation); no team-level insider or aggregation threat.
\end{itemize}
No corpus defense tolerates compromised or colluding \emph{internal} defenders, resists second-order injection from the content it inspects, or is stress-tested by an attacker adapting against the pipeline.

\section*{System and Threat Model}
\textbf{System.} A defense ``agency'': an Input agent, one or more Analyzer agents, a set of Judge agents ($1$--$7$), and an Output agent. The single Coordinator is replaced by a Byzantine-robust aggregation rule over judge verdicts; the inspected payload (a candidate response and/or an inter-agent message) is untrusted. \textbf{Attacker objective:} make the agency pass an unsafe/injected output (integrity) or over-refuse benign outputs (availability). \textbf{Knowledge:} black-box to gray-box; may know the pipeline design; adaptive. \textbf{Capabilities:} (i) compromise/collude with one or more internal defenders; (ii) simultaneously inject the inspected content to attack the judges reading it (second-order injection); (iii) adapt to the aggregation rule. \textbf{Collusion:} colluding defenders coordinate verdicts to flip aggregation. \textbf{Trusted:} the aggregation substrate executes correctly; at least $n-f$ judges are honest (with $2f+2<n$ for a Krum-style rule); the inspected payload is confined to a data channel. \textbf{Out of scope:} all judges compromised; an identical correlated failure across judges (studied as a limiting case, not assumed defended).

\section*{Core Idea}
A \textbf{defense architecture} that tolerates compromised/colluding internal defenders and resists second-order injection from the content it inspects. Four components: (1) \emph{Byzantine-robust verdict aggregation} over defenders --- a Krum/coordinate-median rule replacing the single Coordinator, so a malicious minority cannot flip the decision; (2) \emph{strict isolation} of the untrusted inspected payload from defender instructions (StruQ-style delimiting / structured input) so the judges cannot themselves be injected by what they read; (3) \emph{hardened judge agents} fine-tuned with SecAlign/StruQ so each individually resists injection; (4) \emph{generalization from jailbreak to injection/task-integrity}, extending the verdict target from ``harmful content'' to ``violated the intended task/instruction hierarchy.'' The claim is graceful degradation under insider compromise and second-order injection --- explicitly \emph{not} that reduced ASR equals ``secure.''

\section*{Novelty}
\textbf{Vs.\ closest work.} AutoDefense (arXiv:2403.04783) assumes honest agents, a single coordinator, and only static attacks; Krum (NeurIPS 2017) is robust only under a trusted master; SecAlign (arXiv:2410.05451) and StruQ (arXiv:2402.06363) harden a lone model. \textbf{Not a model-swap / prompt-filter / engineering tweak:} swapping in a hardened judge (component 3 alone) is exactly our single-model baseline; the contribution is (a) removing the trusted-coordinator assumption via Byzantine-robust aggregation over defenders and (b) closing the second-order injection surface the defense creates by reading untrusted text --- neither expressible for one model. \textbf{Fundamentally multi-agent:} the object being defended is a \emph{team} with insider risk and an internal aggregation point. Colluding-defender and coordinator-compromise threats have no single-agent analog; a lone SecAlign model has no co-defender to collude and no aggregator to subvert, and composing per-agent hardened models without robust aggregation addresses neither.

\section*{Expected Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A threat model for \emph{insider} compromise and \emph{second-order} injection inside a defense pipeline --- the first to drop AutoDefense's honest-agent and single-coordinator assumptions.
  \item \texttt{Aegis-Agency}, an architecture combining Byzantine-robust verdict aggregation, payload isolation, and hardened judges, generalized from jailbreak to injection/task-integrity.
  \item An adaptive-attack evaluation against the pipeline (compromised judge, colluding defenders, second-order payload injection, attacks on the aggregation rule) --- absent from prior multi-agent defenses.
  \item A security-vs-cost frontier: Byzantine tolerance fraction vs.\ latency and token/API cost as the number of hardened defenders grows.
  \item A stated diversity requirement and analysis of correlated failure when judges share a base model \hyp.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Preliminary Technical Direction}
\textbf{Flow.} The inspected payload enters a \emph{data} channel isolated (StruQ-style delimiters / structured fields) from each judge's \emph{instruction} channel. Each of $n$ hardened judges emits a verdict $v_k$ (allow/block plus a calibrated score and rationale embedding). \textbf{Robust aggregation.} A Krum/geometric-median selection over $\{v_k\}$ in verdict/embedding space replaces the Coordinator; under the Krum condition $2f+2<n$, up to $f$ Byzantine judges cannot force an adversarial verdict --- a target property to establish for the semantic verdict space, \emph{to be proven}, not assumed. \textbf{Generalization.} Redefine the verdict target from harmful-content classification to task-integrity: did the candidate violate the original instruction/privilege hierarchy? \textbf{Diversity.} Krum tolerance is vacuous if all judges fail identically, so we require backbone/prompt diversity and measure residual correlation. \textbf{Non-claim:} lower ASR-under-compromise is evidence of robustness, not a proof of security; residual failures are reported.

\section*{Evaluation Strategy}
\textbf{LLM families.} Judges and analyzers on open (Llama-3, Qwen2.5, Mistral) and proprietary (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5) backbones, homogeneous and diverse. \textbf{Number of defenders.} $1$--$7$ judges. \textbf{Adversary.} malicious/colluding fraction swept from independent single-compromise to colluding minorities up to the $2f+2<n$ boundary; static vs.\ adaptive attacks on the pipeline and the aggregation rule. \textbf{Attacks.} compromised-judge; colluding-defenders; second-order injection of the inspected payload; adaptive attack on aggregation. \textbf{Baselines.} AutoDefense (single Coordinator); a single hardened model (SecAlign); plain majority vote. \textbf{Metrics.} defense success rate; ASR-under-compromise and targeted-ASR; false-positive/over-refusal rate; Byzantine tolerance fraction; detection F1/AUROC of malicious verdicts; latency (linear in the number of agents --- quantified); token/API cost; utility retention on benign traffic. \textbf{Ablations.} robust-aggregation on/off; payload isolation on/off; hardened judges on/off; number of defenders $1$--$7$; colluding fraction; homogeneous vs.\ diverse backbones (correlated-failure test).

\section*{Potential Risks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cost/latency.} Many hardened judges plus aggregation raise latency and token/API cost; the security-vs-cost frontier may be unattractive at small $n$.
  \item \textbf{Small-committee tolerance.} With $3$--$5$ judges the Krum condition $2f+2<n$ tolerates very few faults ($f=1$ needs $n\ge4$); whether this is meaningful robustness is an open empirical question, not assumed.
  \item \textbf{Correlated failure.} If all judges share a base model they may fail identically, voiding Byzantine tolerance; the required diversity may be hard to achieve \hyp.
  \item \textbf{Utility / interpretation.} Robust aggregation biased toward blocking could raise false positives (over-refusal is measured explicitly); reduced ASR-under-compromise is not ``secure,'' and residual failures are reported.
\end{itemize}
